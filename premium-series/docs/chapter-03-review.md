## Investigation, modelling and mixed review

- **Investigation:** Products 96, 135, 216 match 4×24, 3×45, 6×36. Test coprime 5 and 8 (1×40) and nested 4 and 12 (4×12). For any prime the smaller plus larger exponent equals the sum of the two original exponents. This explains the invariant, rather than relying on agreement in a few examples. For 2,4,8 the product is 64 but HCF×LCM is 16; the middle exponent is missing. For an entry scaffold, count copies for a single prime before transferring the argument. This is suitable as supported extension reasoning, not as routine fluency.
- **Model:** HCF(96,72,60) = 12 stations with 8 cones, 6 batons and 5 bibs each. The first joint finish is 10:24, with clearance at 10:28. Latest start is 09:57 with clearance exactly at closing. Checking 8,9,10,11-minute baton cycles gives joint intervals 8,72,40,88 minutes. Only 8 minutes fits from 10:00 and clears at 10:12. Earlier start preserves both activities but requires venue access; shortening the baton drill may change participation or practice goals. With five minutes’ clearance, the fixed-cycle latest start becomes 09:56; the shortened drill still clears at 10:13. An observed upper bound on clearing times is more useful than assuming one trial is typical. Quantities and times are stipulated scenario data, not measured claims.
- **Mixed review:** 11 divides 77, so 77 is a multiple of 11. The first three-digit multiple of 9 is 108 and its digits are distinct. 121 = 11²; the stopping rule includes 11. The HCF and LCM of 45 and 75 are 15 and 225. With three factors, the middle one must pair with itself, so the number is its square; if that middle factor were composite, a proper factor of it would give an additional divisor.

The chapter progresses from paired factors and direct tests to correcting arguments, exhausting possibilities, generalising and justifying a decision under changed assumptions.
