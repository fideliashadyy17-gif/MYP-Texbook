## 3. Framework rules used in the audit

The consulted guide organises mathematics into four branches:

- Numerical and abstract reasoning.
- Thinking with models.
- Spatial reasoning.
- Reasoning with data.

The topics and skills in the framework are examples, not a mandatory exhaustive syllabus. Schools are not expected to address every branch in every year, but over the programme students should experience all four branches. Extended/enrichment material may be included with school justification; enrichment is optional and not included in eAssessments. This prevents two false audit moves: deleting a revisited topic merely because it appears earlier, and adding advanced content merely to make MYP5 look harder.

Teaching time: at least 50 hours per subject group per year; for MYP certificate course results, at least 70 hours in each of the final two years. The school must confirm actual allocation.

## 4. Objective and criterion progression

The guide prescribes objectives for years 1, 3 and 5; years 2 and 4 require school-developed articulation. All strands of all four objectives must be addressed at least twice each year. A chapter may focus on selected criteria, but the annual map must show coverage.

| Criterion | Year 1 demand | Year 3 demand | Year 5 demand | Audit consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A · Knowing and understanding | Select, apply and solve correctly in familiar/unfamiliar contexts | Same objective, greater mathematical complexity | Same objective, greater complexity and transfer | Check method choice and unfamiliar transfer, not only procedure |
| B · Investigating patterns | Recognise patterns; describe rules; verify with other examples | Discover complex patterns; describe rules; verify and justify | Prove, or verify and justify, general rules | MYP3 must not stop at verification; MYP5 may prove where appropriate |
| C · Communicating | Language, representations, coherent reasoning, logical structure (i–iv) | Adds moving between representations and complete/coherent reasoning; logical structure (i–v) | Complete, coherent and concise reasoning; logical structure (i–v) | MYP1 C i–iv is not an error; MYP3 planner C i–iv omits strand v |
| D · Applying mathematics in real-life contexts | Identify elements, select/apply strategies; explain accuracy; describe whether solution makes sense | Explain accuracy; explain whether solution makes sense | Justify accuracy; justify whether solution makes sense | Do not award D for a dressed-up calculation; require context, accuracy and sense-making |

Use “Criterion”, not “Category”, for assessment. The word “Category” in the books also appears in author credentials, ATL categories and data categories; those are not established assessment misuse.

## 5. Concepts, contexts and ATL

Mathematics contributes the key concepts **Form, Logic, Relationships**. Other MYP key concepts, including **Change**, may be used with a clear interdisciplinary or conceptual rationale. The prescribed related-concept list is: Approximation, Change, Equivalence, Generalization, Models, Patterns, Quantity, Representation, Simplification, Space, Systems, Validity. The guide also permits schools to identify additional related concepts. Therefore:

- `Change` as a key concept is not automatically wrong.
- `Patterns` as a key concept in MYP3 Chapter 2 is outside the prescribed key-concept list; make the local hierarchy explicit or use Form/Relationships with Patterns as related.
- Local related concepts such as Measurement, Justification, Proportion, Statistics and Probability are permitted if defined and purposeful. They should not be presented as prescribed list items.
- Singular local labels such as Pattern, System and Model should be reconciled with the guide’s Patterns, Systems and Models for consistency, unless the school deliberately chooses local vocabulary.

Global contexts must frame inquiry, not decorate a chapter. MYP4 course-map/opener mismatches must be resolved before rewrite. ATL statements must be measurable and use the exact required wording whenever an ATL statement is included:

> In order for a student to… students must…

The required phrase was not found in the five books. ATL labels and “in action” boxes exist, but the second edition needs explicit skill statements, practice opportunities, feedback and evidence.
