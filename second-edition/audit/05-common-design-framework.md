# 05 · Common improvement framework and chapter template

Status: proposed for approval. This framework preserves the first editions’ concept-driven identity while making learning architecture, assessment evidence and production quality explicit. It is not a rule that every short lesson must contain every component.

## 1. Design principles

1. **Mathematics first.** Every feature must earn its place by improving understanding, reasoning, transfer or feedback.
2. **Inquiry with a payoff.** An opening problem should create a need for the chapter’s mathematics and be revisited after learning.
3. **Explanation before compression.** Show why a method works, when it fails, and how representations connect before giving a shortcut.
4. **Practice that changes thinking.** Difficulty increases by reasoning demand, representation change, constraint, error diagnosis and transfer—not merely by larger numbers.
5. **Assessment honesty.** Name the Criterion and strands actually evidenced. Do not force all four criteria into every activity.
6. **Inclusive, credible contexts.** Use Benin, African and international settings with verified data, respectful specificity and no stereotype. Label simulated data.
7. **Visuals that teach.** Diagrams must carry mathematical information: labels, units, scale, correspondence, invariant, counterexample or comparison.
8. **Print-safe production.** Editable sources, reproducible exports, stable cross-references, embedded fonts, accessible contrast and no clipping.

## 2. Common chapter template

Use this as a coherent flow, not a checklist to stamp onto every page.

### A. Opening and inquiry
- Opening problem or phenomenon that students can attempt but not fully solve.
- Statement of inquiry; key concept; related concepts; global context.
- Factual, conceptual and debatable questions.
- Prerequisite check with a short refresher path.
- Learning intentions and success criteria written as observable performances.

### B. Exploration
- A short investigation, comparison, measurement, simulation or counterexample search.
- Students record conjectures, cases, representations and limitations.
- Teacher notes identify likely misconceptions and decision points.
- Criterion B evidence only when students select/apply techniques, describe a rule and verify/justify/prove as appropriate to the year.

### C. Mathematical development
- Definitions with non-examples and boundary cases.
- Multiple representations: words, tables, graphs, diagrams, symbols, technology where useful.
- Key idea boxes that state a general principle, not a slogan.
- Explicit conditions: domain, units, assumptions, fairness, independence, open/closed surfaces, exact vs approximate.

### D. Reasoned worked examples
Each major method should have at least one example with:
1. Problem and given information.
2. Method choice: why this tool fits.
3. Step-by-step reasoning with justification.
4. Check: substitution, inverse operation, estimate, diagram or unit analysis.
5. Interpretation: what the answer means and when it would fail.
6. Common error or alternative method where useful.

### E. Scaffolded → independent practice
- Guided completion with fading support.
- Fluency: accurate procedure and notation.
- Understanding: explain, classify, represent, compare.
- Reasoning: justify, prove, find counterexamples, diagnose errors.
- Application: multi-step, contextual, constraint-based.
- Extension/transfer: unfamiliar setting, method choice, open-ended investigation.
- Mixed retrieval: earlier topics interleaved.

Challenge tiers must increase reasoning. A longer calculation is not automatically a higher tier.

### F. Transfer and modelling
- Authentic or clearly simulated context with data provenance.
- Variables, units, assumptions, constraints and objective.
- Model build, solution, validation, sensitivity and limitation.
- Criterion D evidence only when students identify elements, select/apply strategies, justify accuracy and justify whether the solution makes sense.

### G. Criterion practice and feedback
- Short formative checks aligned to specific strands.
- One substantial criterion task or milestone per chapter where appropriate.
- Student-facing rubric written in plain language, explicitly not an official IB descriptor unless approved.
- Sample responses at different achievement levels with annotation.
- Feedback prompt: what to repair next.

### H. Summary and reflection
- Key ideas and method-choice map.
- Self-check with answers or success criteria.
- Reflection: What changed in your thinking? What error did you repair? Where else does this apply?
- ATL reflection tied to an observable skill.

## 3. ATL integration rule

Whenever an ATL statement is included, use exactly:

> In order for a student to… students must…

Example:

> In order for a student to justify a general rule from a pattern, students must test cases beyond the generating examples, state the rule precisely, and explain why the rule holds or verify it against independent cases.

Each ATL statement needs:
- a skill category and specific indicator;
- a task where it is practised;
- feedback or self-assessment evidence;
- a reflection prompt.

Do not add ATL labels as decoration.

## 4. Exercise-type bank

Use a balanced mix across a chapter:

| Type | Purpose | Example stem |
|---|---|---|
| Fluency | Accuracy and automaticity | Calculate/simplify/solve… |
| Missing information | Structure awareness | Find the missing value that makes… |
| Matching | Representation connection | Match table, graph, equation and story… |
| Error diagnosis | Misconception repair | A student says… Find the error and correct it… |
| Counterexample | Boundary reasoning | Show the claim is false by… |
| Method choice | Strategic knowledge | Which method is best here? Why? |
| Explanation | Criterion C | Explain why… using words and symbols… |
| Investigation | Criterion B | Explore… State a rule… Verify/justify/prove… |
| Modelling | Criterion D | Design/plan/evaluate… State assumptions and accuracy… |
| Retrieval | Durability | Mixed problem from earlier chapters… |
| Technology | Tool literacy | Use a spreadsheet/graphing tool to… Interpret, do not just output… |

## 5. Visual and identity system

Preserve first-edition identity rather than replacing it with a generic template.

- MYP1–3 credits: Fraunces, Source Serif 4, Inter; KaTeX; original vector art; HTML/CSS paged-media workflow.
- MYP4–5 credits: Source Serif 4, Inter, Bodoni Moda; KaTeX; original vector graphics; A4.
- Recover the actual colour palette, cover geometry, chapter-opener rhythm and icon language from the first editions before setting second-edition tokens. Do not invent a new brand palette without approval.
- Use colour consistently by function: inquiry, key idea, example, practice, assessment, reflection. Ensure greyscale readability.
- Diagrams need source files, labels, units, scale where relevant, and a caption that states what to notice.
- Geometry diagrams must not imply unsupported generality. Mark given information; do not mark conclusions as given.
- Statistical graphs need axes, units, source, sample size and honest scales.
- All diagrams must be redrawable from editable sources; PDF-only vector extraction is not a production plan.

## 6. Context and inclusion policy

- Benin contexts may include CFA francs, local markets, transport, water access, agriculture, crafts, architecture, school life and public data—but only with verified or clearly simulated values.
- African contexts should be varied and specific, not a single “Africa” stereotype.
- International contexts should include multiple regions and histories of mathematics.
- Names, currencies, units and scenarios should be respectful and mathematically necessary.
- Real data needs source, date, unit, collection method and limitation. Simulated data must be labelled.
- Avoid contexts that require expensive equipment unless a low-cost alternative is provided.
- Provide multiple entry points: visual, verbal, numeric, collaborative and independent.

## 7. Readability and accessibility

- Short paragraphs, predictable hierarchy, generous whitespace.
- Define technical terms at first use; glossary entries match usage.
- Mathematical notation consistent across levels: variables, units, rounding, set notation, function notation, probability notation.
- Avoid dense pages that mix too many new symbols.
- Alt text/descriptions for diagrams in editable sources.
- Colour contrast and greyscale tests.
- Print test at actual A4 size; no clipped captions, equations or answer text.

## 8. Prototype acceptance criteria

The MYP1 prototype should demonstrate:
- one complete chapter with the template used flexibly;
- one investigation with genuine Criterion B evidence;
- one contextual task with genuine Criterion D evidence;
- reasoned worked examples and error diagnosis;
- progressive practice tiers;
- exact ATL wording and evidence;
- first-edition-derived visual tokens;
- editable source and reproducible PDF export;
- QA ledger with question/answer/diagram/export checks.
