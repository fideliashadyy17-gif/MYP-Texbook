7.1 Lines, angles and angle facts

Big idea

Name the relationship first: straight line, full turn, vertically opposite or right angle.

An angle measures a turn. A half-turn along a straight line is 180°, so adjacent angles on a straight line are supplementary. A full turn about a point is 360°, so angles around a point sum to 360°. When two straight lines cross, the angles opposite each other are vertically opposite and equal.

Figure 7.1 · The angle x and 65° lie on a straight line, so x+65°=180°.

Classify angles as acute (<90°), right (90°), obtuse (between 90° and 180°), straight (180°) or reflex (between 180° and 360°). A diagram is not always drawn to scale, so use stated facts rather than appearance.

Key idea

Straight line: 180°. Full turn: 360°. Vertically opposite: equal. Right angle: 90°. The middle letter of ∠ABC names the vertex B.

7.1 Worked examples and guided practice

Worked example 7.1.1 · Missing angle on a line

Two adjacent angles on a straight line are 47° and x. Find x.

  1. Angles on a straight line sum to 180°.
  2. x = 180−47 = 133°.
  3. Check: 47+133=180.
x=133°.
Worked example 7.1.2 · Angles around a point

Four angles meet at a point: 95°, 120°, 85° and x. Find x.

  1. Angles around a point sum to 360°.
  2. x = 360−(95+120+85) = 60°.
  3. Check: 95+120+85+60=360.
x=60°.
Common error

Do not swap the totals: a straight line is 180°, a full turn is 360°. Do not treat vertically opposite angles as supplementary; they are equal. The adjacent pair is supplementary.

Guided practice · copy and complete

Angles on a straight line: 118° + □ = 180°, so the missing angle is □°. Angles at a point: 95° + 120° + 85° + □ = 360°, so the missing angle is □°. Vertically opposite: if one angle is 47°, the opposite angle is □°, and each adjacent angle is 180−□ = □°.

7.2 Parallel lines and triangles

A transversal is a line that crosses two or more other lines. When the crossed lines are parallel, the eight angles formed fall into equal or supplementary families.

Figure 7.2 · With l ∥ m: corresponding and alternate angles are equal; co-interior angles sum to 180°.

Key idea

Corresponding angles sit in the same position at each intersection and are equal. Alternate angles lie on opposite sides of the transversal between the parallel lines and are equal. Co-interior angles lie on the same side between the parallel lines and are supplementary.

Watch for this misconception

Do not assume lines are parallel or angles equal unless marked or stated. “Looks like 90°” is not a reason.

Triangle sums and exterior angles

Every triangle has an angle sum of 180°. This follows from the parallel-line facts. Triangles are classified by sides as equilateral, isosceles or scalene, and by angles as acute, right or obtuse. In an isosceles triangle the angles opposite the equal sides are equal.

Figure 7.3 · Equal sides force equal base angles.
Worked example 7.2.1 · Isosceles base angles

An isosceles triangle has apex angle 40°. Find each base angle.

  1. Base angles are equal; call each x.
  2. 2x+40=180, so 2x=140.
  3. x=70°.
Each base angle is 70°.

An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles. It is supplementary to the interior angle at the same vertex.

7.3 Polygons and their angles

A polygon is a closed plane shape made from straight sides. Choose one vertex and draw diagonals to every non-adjacent vertex. The polygon is divided into triangles. An n-sided polygon divides into n−2 triangles, so its interior angle sum is (n−2)×180°.

Figure 7.4 · A hexagon splits into four triangles; the interior angle sum is 4×180°=720°.
Worked example 7.3.1 · Regular polygon

A regular polygon has exterior angle 45°. Find the number of sides and each interior angle.

  1. Exterior angles of any convex polygon sum to 360°.
  2. n = 360÷45 = 8.
  3. Interior = 180−45 = 135°.
8 sides; each interior angle 135°.

Key idea

Interior sum: (n−2)×180°. Exterior sum: 360°. Regular polygon: each exterior = 360÷n; each interior = 180−360÷n.

7.4 Worked construction and guided steps

Worked example 7.4.1 · SSS triangle

Construct a triangle with sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm.

  1. Draw base AB=7 cm.
  2. Open the compass to 5 cm; draw an arc from A.
  3. Open the compass to 6 cm; draw an arc from B.
  4. Label the intersection C and join AC and BC.
  5. Check: 5+6>7, so the arcs meet above the base.
Triangle ABC has the required three sides. Keep the arcs visible.
Common error

Never change the compass between a pair of arcs. If the radius is not greater than half the segment, the arcs for a perpendicular bisector will not meet. Check the defining property, not the appearance of the drawing.

Guided practice · order the steps

Put these steps for a perpendicular bisector in order: (i) keep the same radius and draw arcs from A and B; (ii) draw segment AB; (iii) draw the line through the two intersection points; (iv) open the compasses to more than half of AB. Correct order: __, __, __, __. The line is perpendicular because both intersection points are the same distance from __ and from __.

Chapter 7 · Investigation · Discovery and conclusion

From arcs to a rule

Discovery

The two shorter sides must together be strictly greater than the longest side. If a+b=c, the arcs meet on the base and the triangle has zero area. If a+b<c, the arcs do not meet.

Conclusion

For sides a, b, c with c longest, a triangle exists if and only if a+b>c. The other two inequalities then hold automatically. This is the triangle inequality.

Summary and connections

Angle facts

  • Straight line: 180°.
  • Full turn: 360°.
  • Vertically opposite: equal.
  • Parallel lines: corresponding and alternate equal; co-interior supplementary.

Triangles and polygons

  • Triangle sum: 180°.
  • Exterior angle: sum of opposite interior angles.
  • n-gon interior sum: (n−2)×180°.
  • Exterior sum: 360°.

Constructions

  • Perpendicular bisector: equal radii greater than half the segment.
  • Angle bisector: equal arcs from the arms.
  • SSS triangle: arcs meet when the triangle inequality holds.

Inquiry

  • Triangle inequality: two shorter sides > longest.
  • Equality gives a degenerate straight line.
  • Keep arcs visible as evidence.
Connections · Drawing is not proving

A construction is an argument made with instruments. Each arc records a fixed distance from a named point, so the finished drawing already contains evidence: equal radii, equal distances, and a property that can be checked. A picture that looks right is not enough; the defining relationship must hold.

Where this leads: Chapter 8 turns classification into a precise language. Sets describe which objects share a property, and Venn diagrams show relationships of overlap and exclusion — the same kind of structural thinking used to sort triangles and angles here. Chapter 9 then measures the shapes whose boundaries you have learned to reason about.
