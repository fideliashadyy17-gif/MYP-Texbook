# MYP1 master correction · Stage 1 comparative audit

**Status of current Second Edition proofs: NOT APPROVED.**  
**Date:** 2026-11-02  
**Scope:** MYP1 Chapters 1–10 only. MYP2 has not been started.  
**Method:** fresh comparison of the untouched First Edition PDFs, the current unapproved Second Edition PDFs, and verified IB sources listed in `source-register.md`. Evidence images and metrics are in `evidence/`.

---

## 1. Executive judgement

The current proofs are mechanically sound but editorially weaker than the First Edition. They pass layout/PDF/math checks, yet they do not meet the user’s standard for a premium MYP coursebook. The main failures are:

1. **Teaching density and development were reduced.** Across Chapters 1–10, the First Edition chapter bodies contain about **85,950 words**; the current proofs contain about **45,585 words including answer pages**. Word count is only a density proxy, but the gap matches the visible loss of explanations, guided practice, worked examples and connective tissue.
2. **Worked modelling was cut.** First Edition worked-example headings: **106**. Current proofs: **57**.
3. **Scaffolded “copy and complete” practice was largely removed.** First Edition guided-practice headings: **39**. Current proofs replace these with short generic prompts.
4. **Exercise banks are not visual mathematics.** The current proofs contain **628 exercise items** but **0 structured item-level visual assets**. Several prompts even refer to diagrams that are not supplied. This directly violates the required 30–40% visual exercise target.
5. **Chapter openers lost their subject-specific identity.** The First Edition opener for Chapter 7 combines a truss illustration, an inquiry paragraph, concept chips and a composed dark-green layout. The current proof opener is a generic numeral/title page with large empty space and no topic illustration.
6. **Inquiry is often announced rather than enacted.** In Chapter 7, the current proof states the triangle-inequality rule before asking students to “complete the table”. The First Edition uses compass arcs, a testing table, borderline cases and an explanation task to let students discover the condition.
7. **The design is cleaner but less professional as a textbook.** The current proofs have consistent headers/footers and callouts, but they use a repeated template, thin content pages, and proof labels. They do not yet feel like a finished premium coursebook.

**Decision:** do not roll out the current template. Rebuild one representative chapter from the First Edition’s strengths, then stop for approval.

## 4. First Edition strengths to restore

1. **Composed chapter openers**: dark-green identity, topic-specific mathematical illustration, inquiry paragraph, concept chips, restrained gold/rust accents.
2. **Explicit teaching sequence**: big idea → explanation → diagram → worked example → common error → guided practice → independent practice.
3. **Guided copy-and-complete scaffolds** before independent work.
4. **Multiple worked examples per major section**, including checks and alternative representations.
5. **Practice sets with support/core/extension logic**, not only tier labels.
6. **Construction clinic / method tables** that tie procedure to evidence.
7. **Investigations that withhold the rule until students have tested cases**, including borderline cases and justification.
8. **Real-life applications with constraints, scale and evaluation**, not just word-problem wrappers.
9. **Criterion-mapped tasks** that assess only the criteria they genuinely evidence.
10. **Mixed practice and progress checks** with reasons, self-rating and forward links.
11. **Visual identity**: cream paper, deep green, gold, rust; serif display type; mathematical line art; consistent figure captions.

## 5. Current proof strengths to keep

- Editable HTML/CSS/SVG/JSON source pipeline.
- Automated build, layout, PDF and mathematics QA.
- Explicit tier labels and answer reasoning.
- Misconception callouts.
- Some improved local/international contexts.
- Clean headers/footers and pagination mechanics.

These are production strengths. They do not compensate for the editorial losses.

## 7. Five-year progression contract for geometry

The prototype must respect the existing five-year arc without importing DP content.

| Level | Geometry demand in the First Editions | Prototype boundary |
|---|---|---|
| MYP1 | Angle facts, parallel lines, triangle/polygon sums, ruler-and-compass constructions, perimeter/area/volume | Reason with given facts and equal radii; no trigonometry, no formal similarity proofs |
| MYP2 | 2D/3D measure, transformations, symmetry | MYP1 constructions prepare for later transformation reasoning |
| MYP3 | Congruence, similarity, Pythagoras, trig ratios, solids | MYP1 must not teach Pythagoras/trig early |
| MYP4 | Further congruence/similarity/transformations, Pythagoras/trig, surface area/volume | MYP1 stays concrete and construction-based |
| MYP5 | Circle proof, sine/cosine rules, 3D trig, vectors/matrices (pathway-dependent) | MYP1 must not fake advancement with circle theorems or vectors |

**Progression contract:** MYP1 establishes angle facts and construction evidence; MYP2 adds transformation and symmetry; MYP3 adds congruence/similarity and right-triangle trigonometry; MYP4–5 extend to non-right trigonometry, proof and pathway-dependent topics. Repetition is purposeful only when demand increases.

## 8. Correction decisions for the prototype

1. **Keep the First Edition chapter sequence and mathematical order.** Do not simplify the topic into a worksheet.
2. **Restore teaching density.** Each major section needs explanation, diagram, worked example, misconception, guided scaffold and independent practice.
3. **Rebuild the exercise bank.** Target 35 meaningful items/subquestions, not 55 thin rephrasings. Include fluency, conceptual, diagram/graph/table, reasoning, error correction, true/false with justification, MCQ, missing-step, multi-step, real-life and open challenge.
4. **Make visual mathematics mandatory.** Target 12 of 35 items (≈34%) with accurate labelled SVG diagrams. Graphs/diagrams must have axes/scales/labels/units where relevant. No decorative-only figures.
5. **Rebuild inquiry as discovery.** Engagement → inquiry questions → investigation → observations → discovery → conclusion. Do not state the triangle inequality before students test arcs and borderline cases.
6. **Restore premium opener and cover language.** Topic-specific truss/construction illustration; deep green/cream/gold/rust; no “Oak International School”, no school-as-publisher, no invented publisher/ISBN/date/author credentials.
7. **Use criteria honestly.** Chapter 7 can evidence A, B, C and D in separate tasks, but not force every criterion into every exercise.
8. **Use exact ATL wording:** “In order for a student to… students must…”.
9. **Keep answers student-facing.** Reasoning and checks, not teacher-only marking commentary.
10. **Stop after the prototype.** No rollout to Chapters 1–6 or 8–10, and no MYP2, until the user approves.

## 9. Prototype selection

**Selected chapter: MYP1 Chapter 7 — Lines, angles, triangles and constructions.**

Rationale:

- It is visually rich and therefore tests the 30–40% visual exercise requirement honestly.
- It has a natural inquiry: when do three lengths close a triangle?
- It combines reasoning, construction, communication and real-life application.
- It is central to the MYP1–MYP5 geometry progression.
- The First Edition provides a strong reference model, and the current proof shows the exact failures to correct.

A detailed prototype brief is in `prototype-brief.md`. The prototype will be built in a separate directory so the unapproved proofs remain untouched for comparison.
