7.1 Lines, angles and angle facts

Name the relationship first: straight line, full turn, vertically opposite or right angle.

An angle measures a turn. A half-turn along a straight line is 180°, so adjacent angles on a straight line are supplementary. A full turn about a point is 360°. When two straight lines cross, vertically opposite angles are equal.

Straight line: the two angles complete 180°.
Full turn: the four angles complete 360°.

Key idea

Straight line: 180°. Full turn: 360°. Vertically opposite: equal. Right angle: 90°. Complementary angles sum to 90°; supplementary angles sum to 180°. The middle letter of angle ABC names the vertex B.

Common error

Do not swap the totals: a straight line is 180°, a full turn is 360°. Do not treat vertically opposite angles as supplementary; they are equal. The adjacent pair is supplementary.

Chapter 7.1 · Worked example 7.1.1

Missing angle on a straight line

Problem

Two adjacent angles on a straight line are 47° and x. Find x.

Visual representation
Figure 7.1 · The two angles share a vertex and lie on a straight line.
Mathematical reasoning

A straight line is a half-turn, so adjacent angles on it are supplementary: their sum is 180°.

Step-by-step solution
  1. Identify the relationship: the two angles lie on one straight line.
  2. Write the equation: 47° + x = 180°.
  3. Subtract 47° from both sides: x = 180° − 47° = 133°.
  4. Check: 47° + 133° = 180°.
Final answer. x = 133°.

Interpretation. The unknown angle is obtuse. The diagram and the equation agree: the two angles complete a half-turn.

Chapter 7.1 · Worked example 7.1.2

Angles around a point

Problem

Four angles meet at a point: 95°, 120°, 85° and x. Find x.

Visual representation
Figure 7.2 · Four sectors fill one full turn.
Mathematical reasoning

A full turn around a point is 360°. The four angles must fill the turn exactly.

Step-by-step solution
  1. Add the known angles: 95° + 120° + 85° = 300°.
  2. Subtract from the full turn: x = 360° − 300° = 60°.
  3. Check: 95° + 120° + 85° + 60° = 360°.
Final answer. x = 60°.

Interpretation. The missing sector is acute. The visual model shows why the total must be 360°, not 180°.

Chapter 7.1 · Worked example 7.1.3

Vertically opposite angles with algebra

Problem

Two lines cross. Vertically opposite angles are labelled 3x + 10° and x + 50°. Find x and one adjacent angle.

Visual representation
Figure 7.3 · Opposite angles are equal; adjacent angles sum to 180°.
Mathematical reasoning

Vertically opposite angles are equal. Adjacent angles on a straight line are supplementary.

Step-by-step solution
  1. Set the opposite angles equal: 3x + 10 = x + 50.
  2. Collect terms: 2x = 40, so x = 20°.
  3. Each marked angle is 3(20) + 10 = 70°.
  4. An adjacent angle is 180° − 70° = 110°. Check: 70° + 110° = 180°.
Final answer. x = 20°; marked angles 70°; adjacent angle 110°.

Interpretation. The algebra confirms the visual symmetry: opposite sectors match, and neighbouring sectors complete a line.

7.2 Parallel lines and transversals

A transversal crosses two or more lines. When the crossed lines are parallel, the angles fall into equal or supplementary families.

Figure 7.4 · With l parallel to m: corresponding and alternate angles are equal; co-interior angles are supplementary.

Key idea

Corresponding angles sit in the same position at each intersection. Alternate angles lie on opposite sides of the transversal between the parallel lines. Co-interior angles lie on the same side between the parallel lines.

Watch for this misconception

Do not assume lines are parallel or angles equal unless marked or stated. Conversely, equal alternate angles or supplementary co-interior angles can show that two lines are parallel.

Triangle and exterior-angle facts

Every triangle has angle sum 180°. An exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.

Equal sides give equal base angles.
Exterior angle = sum of opposite interior angles.

Key idea

Triangles may be classified by sides (equilateral, isosceles, scalene) and by angles (acute, right, obtuse). In an isosceles triangle the angles opposite the equal sides are equal; conversely, equal angles face equal sides. In a right triangle the two acute angles are complementary.

Common error

The exterior angle is equal to the sum of the two opposite interior angles. It is not added to them to make 180°. The adjacent interior angle is the one supplementary to the exterior angle.

Chapter 7.2 · Worked example 7.2.1

Parallel-line angle chase

Problem

Lines l and m are parallel. The marked angle is 68°. Find a, b and c.

Visual representation
Figure 7.5 · Parallel marks justify the angle relationships.
Mathematical reasoning

With parallel lines, alternate angles are equal, co-interior angles are supplementary, and corresponding angles are equal.

Step-by-step solution
  1. a is alternate to 68°, so a = 68°.
  2. b is co-interior with 68°, so b = 180° − 68° = 112°.
  3. c corresponds to the 68° angle (both are in the lower-right position), so c = 68°.
  4. Check: a + b = 68° + 112° = 180°.
Final answer. a = 68°, b = 112°, c = 68°.

Interpretation. The transversal creates two angle families: 68° and 112°. Naming the relationship prevents guessing from appearance.

Chapter 7.2 · Worked example 7.2.2

Exterior angle of a triangle

Problem

An exterior angle of a triangle is 115°. One opposite interior angle is 48°. Find the other opposite interior angle and the adjacent interior angle.

Visual representation
Figure 7.6 · The exterior angle is outside the triangle at the extended base.
Mathematical reasoning

An exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles. It is supplementary to the interior angle at the same vertex.

Step-by-step solution
  1. Other opposite interior angle = 115° − 48° = 67°.
  2. Adjacent interior angle = 180° − 115° = 65°.
  3. Check the triangle sum: 48° + 67° + 65° = 180°.
Final answer. Other opposite angle 67°; adjacent interior angle 65°.

Interpretation. The exterior angle is larger than either opposite interior angle. The diagram shows which angles belong to the exterior-angle relationship.

Chapter 7.2 · Worked example 7.2.3

Isosceles base angles

Problem

An isosceles triangle has apex angle 40°. Find each base angle.

Visual representation
Figure 7.7 · Equal sides force equal base angles.
Mathematical reasoning

Equal sides have equal opposite angles. The triangle angle sum is 180°.

Step-by-step solution
  1. The tick marks show two equal sides, so the base angles are equal. Call each x.
  2. Write 2x + 40° = 180°.
  3. Then 2x = 140°, so x = 70°.
  4. Check: 70° + 70° + 40° = 180°.
Final answer. Each base angle is 70°.

Interpretation. The equal-side marks are the evidence for equal base angles; the calculation then uses the angle sum.

Chapter 7.2 · Worked example 7.2.4

Why every triangle has angle sum 180°

Problem

Use a line through the apex parallel to the base to explain why the angles of a triangle sum to 180°.

Visual representation
Figure 7.8 · The parallel line transfers the base angles to the apex.
Mathematical reasoning

Alternate angles between parallel lines are equal. Angles on a straight line sum to 180°.

Step-by-step solution
  1. Draw a line through the apex parallel to the base.
  2. The left base angle equals the left angle at the apex (alternate angles). The right base angle equals the right angle at the apex.
  3. The three angles on the straight line through the apex are therefore the three triangle angles.
  4. They sum to 180°, so the triangle angles sum to 180°.
Final answer. A + B + C = 180° for every triangle.

Interpretation. This is a visual argument, not a measurement of one triangle. It explains why the rule is general.

7.3 Polygons and their angles

A polygon is a closed plane shape made from straight sides. Its interior angle sum depends on the number of sides.

Figure 7.9 · A hexagon splits into four triangles from one vertex.

Choose one vertex and draw diagonals to every non-adjacent vertex. An n-sided polygon divides into n−2 triangles, so its interior angle sum is (n−2)×180°. The exterior angles of any convex polygon sum to 360°.

Key idea

Interior sum: (n−2)×180°. Exterior sum: 360°. Regular polygon: each exterior = 360°÷n; each interior = 180°−360°÷n.

Chapter 7.3 · Worked example 7.3.1

Missing angle in a quadrilateral

Problem

A quadrilateral has angles 85°, 95°, 110° and x. Find x.

Visual representation
Figure 7.10 · One diagonal divides the quadrilateral into two triangles.
Mathematical reasoning

A quadrilateral can be split into two triangles, so its interior angle sum is 2 × 180° = 360°.

Step-by-step solution
  1. Add the known angles: 85° + 95° + 110° = 290°.
  2. Subtract from 360°: x = 360° − 290° = 70°.
  3. Check: 85° + 95° + 110° + 70° = 360°.
Final answer. x = 70°.

Interpretation. The diagonal in the diagram shows where the 360° comes from: two triangle sums.

Chapter 7.3 · Worked example 7.3.2

Regular polygon from an exterior angle

Problem

A regular polygon has exterior angle 40°. Find the number of sides and each interior angle.

Visual representation
Figure 7.11 · A regular nonagon has nine equal exterior turns.
Mathematical reasoning

The exterior angles of any convex polygon sum to 360°. In a regular polygon they are equal. Interior and exterior angles at a vertex are supplementary.

Step-by-step solution
  1. Number of sides n = 360° ÷ 40° = 9.
  2. Each interior angle = 180° − 40° = 140°.
  3. Check with the interior-sum formula: (9 − 2) × 180° = 1260°, and 1260° ÷ 9 = 140°.
Final answer. 9 sides; each interior angle 140°.

Interpretation. A small exterior angle means many sides. The polygon is a regular nonagon.

Discovery

The two shorter sides must together be strictly greater than the longest side. If a+b=c, the arcs meet on the base and the triangle has zero area. If a+b<c, the arcs do not meet.

Conclusion

For sides a, b, c with c longest, a triangle exists if and only if a+b>c. The other two inequalities then hold automatically. This is the triangle inequality.

Possible third sides

Problem

Two sides of a triangle are 7 cm and 11 cm. The third side is a whole number of centimetres. Find all possible values.

Visual representation
Figure 7.13 · The number line represents 4 < x < 18.
Mathematical reasoning

The third side must be greater than the difference of the other two sides and less than their sum. Endpoints give degenerate cases.

Step-by-step solution
  1. Lower boundary: 11 − 7 = 4, so x > 4.
  2. Upper boundary: 11 + 7 = 18, so x < 18.
  3. Whole-number values are 5, 6, 7, …, 17.
  4. Check endpoints: x = 4 gives 4 + 7 = 11; x = 18 gives 7 + 11 = 18. Both are straight-line cases, not triangles.
Final answer. x = 5, 6, 7, …, 17 cm.

Interpretation. The open circles on the number line show that 4 and 18 are excluded. The integer dots are the possible whole-centimetre lengths.

To construct a triangle from three sides, fix the base and locate the apex with two arcs.

Chapter 7.4 · Worked example 7.4.1

Perpendicular bisector

Problem

Construct the perpendicular bisector of AB = 8 cm. State the midpoint length and the check.

Visual representation
Figure 7.15 · The same compass radius is used from both endpoints.
Mathematical reasoning

Points on the perpendicular bisector are equidistant from A and B. Equal-radius arcs locate two such points.

Step-by-step solution
  1. Draw AB = 8 cm.
  2. Choose one radius greater than half of AB, for example 5 cm. Keep it fixed.
  3. Draw arcs from A and from B above and below AB. Label the intersections P and Q.
  4. Draw PQ. It crosses AB at its midpoint M, so AM = MB = 4 cm, and PQ is perpendicular to AB.
Final answer. AM = MB = 4 cm; PQ is perpendicular to AB.

Interpretation. The arcs are evidence: P and Q are each the same distance from A and B, so the line through them is the perpendicular bisector.

Chapter 7.4 · Worked example 7.4.2

Angle bisector

Problem

Bisect a 64° angle with ruler and compasses. Find each half.

Visual representation
Figure 7.16 · The bisector is the ray OR.
Mathematical reasoning

An angle bisector divides an angle into two equal angles. Equal-radius arcs from the arms locate a point on the bisector.

Step-by-step solution
  1. Draw an arc from the vertex O, cutting the arms at P and Q.
  2. With the same radius from P and Q, draw arcs meeting at R.
  3. Draw OR. This is the angle bisector.
  4. Each half is 64° ÷ 2 = 32°. Check: 32° + 32° = 64°.
Final answer. Each half is 32°.

Interpretation. The construction forces equality; measuring is only a check. Do not confuse this with the perpendicular bisector of a segment.

Chapter 7.4 · Worked example 7.4.3

Triangle from three sides (SSS)

Problem

Construct a triangle with sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm.

Visual representation
Figure 7.17 · The arc intersection fixes the third vertex.
Mathematical reasoning

If two sides and the base are fixed, the third vertex is the intersection of two circles/arcs. The triangle inequality predicts whether the arcs meet.

Step-by-step solution
  1. Check: 5 + 6 > 7, so a triangle is possible.
  2. Draw base AB = 7 cm.
  3. From A draw an arc of radius 5 cm; from B draw an arc of radius 6 cm.
  4. Label the intersection C and join AC and BC. Keep the arcs visible.
Final answer. Triangle ABC has AB = 7 cm, AC = 5 cm and BC = 6 cm.

Interpretation. The arcs locate the only possible apex on each side of the base. The two choices are mirror images.

Chapter 7.4 · Worked example 7.4.4

Isosceles triangle and a locus

Problem

Construct an isosceles triangle with equal sides 6 cm and base 8 cm. Explain why the perpendicular bisector of the base passes through the apex.

Visual representation
Figure 7.18 · Equal sides place the apex on the perpendicular bisector.
Mathematical reasoning

The apex is equidistant from the base endpoints. The perpendicular bisector is the locus of all points equidistant from the endpoints.

Step-by-step solution
  1. Draw base AB = 8 cm.
  2. With radius 6 cm from A and from B, draw arcs meeting at C.
  3. Join AC and BC. Then AC = BC = 6 cm.
  4. Because C is equidistant from A and B, it lies on the perpendicular bisector of AB.
Final answer. The perpendicular bisector of AB passes through C.

Interpretation. The equal sides are not just a picture feature; they place the apex on a precise locus. This connects construction with reasoning.

Summary and connections

Angle facts

  • Straight line: 180°.
  • Full turn: 360°.
  • Vertically opposite: equal.
  • Parallel lines: corresponding and alternate equal; co-interior supplementary.

Triangles and polygons

  • Triangle sum: 180°.
  • Exterior angle: sum of opposite interior angles.
  • n-gon interior sum: (n−2)×180°.
  • Exterior sum: 360°.

Constructions

  • Perpendicular bisector: equal radii greater than half the segment.
  • Angle bisector: equal arcs from the arms.

Connections · Drawing is not proving

A construction is an argument made with instruments. Each arc records a fixed distance from a named point, so the finished drawing already contains evidence: equal radii, equal distances, and a property that can be checked. A picture that looks right is not enough; the defining relationship must hold.
