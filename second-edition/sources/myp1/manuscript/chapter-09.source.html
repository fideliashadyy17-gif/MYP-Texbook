Chapter 9 · Mathematical development

9.1 Perimeter and area of triangles and quadrilaterals

Big idea

Choose the quantity the task needs: boundary length, covered surface or space inside.

Perimeter is the total boundary length. Area measures surface in square units. Rectangle area = length×width; triangle area = ½×base×perpendicular height; parallelogram area = base×height.

A trapezoid with parallel sides a and b and perpendicular height h has area ½(a+b)h. The height must be perpendicular to the parallel sides, not a slant edge.

{{figure:rectangle_area-9|Area counts square units (28 cm²); perimeter counts boundary units (22 cm).}}
Worked example · A triangle and a trapezoid

Triangle: base 8 cm, height 5 cm. Trapezoid: parallel sides 6 and 10 cm, height 4 cm.

  1. Triangle: ½×8×5 = 20 cm².
  2. Trapezoid: ½×(6+10)×4 = 32 cm².
20 cm² and 32 cm².

Chapter 9 · Mathematical development

9.2 Circles and composite shapes

Big idea

Identify radius and diameter, then split a composite shape into familiar pieces.

For a circle, diameter d=2r. Circumference C=πd=2πr; area A=πr². In this chapter use π≈3.14 unless told otherwise, and label results as approximate.

A semicircle has half the circle area and a curved boundary of πr. For a composite plan, add areas of non-overlapping pieces and trace only the outer boundary for fencing.

{{figure:circle-9|d = 2r. Area uses r²; circumference uses 2r. Use π ≈ 3.14 here.}}
Worked example · A semicircular stage

Diameter 12 m, so radius 6 m.

  1. Area = ½×3.14×6² = 56.52 m².
  2. Curved boundary = 3.14×6 = 18.84 m.
Area ≈56.52 m²; arc ≈18.84 m.

Chapter 9 · Mathematical development

9.3 Nets and surface area

Big idea

A net shows every outside face; surface area adds face areas, not the space inside.

A net is a flat pattern that folds into a solid. The surface area of a closed cuboid is 2(lw+lh+wh). For an open-topped box, omit the top face.

Use square units for surface area. Painting an outside surface depends on area; filling the box depends on volume. Keep the two quantities separate.

{{figure:cuboid_net-9|A net exposes all six faces. Surface area = 2(lw + lh + wh).}}
Worked example · An open-topped raised bed

Length 1.2 m, width 0.6 m, height 0.5 m.

  1. Base: 1.2×0.6 = 0.72 m².
  2. Two long sides: 2×1.2×0.5 = 1.20 m². Two short sides: 2×0.6×0.5 = 0.60 m².
Outside area = 2.52 m².

Chapter 9 · Mathematical development

9.4 Prisms, volume and capacity

Big idea

Volume measures space; capacity measures how much a container can hold.

A prism has a constant cross-section. Volume = cross-section area × length. For a cuboid, V=lwh; for a triangular prism, V=(½×base×height)×length.

Capacity conversions: 1 cm³ = 1 mL; 1,000 cm³ = 1 L; 1 m³ = 1,000 L. Convert units before multiplying or dividing.

{{figure:prism-9|Volume counts cubic units: 5 × 3 × 2 = 30 cm³ = 30 mL.}}
Worked example · A tank and a prism

Tank 50 cm × 30 cm × 40 cm; triangular prism with cross-section base 8 cm, height 5 cm, length 14 cm.

  1. Tank volume 60,000 cm³ = 60 L.
  2. Prism volume ½×8×5×14 = 280 cm³.
60 L; 280 cm³.
Chapter 9 · Practice

9.4 Guided and independent practice

Watch for this misconception

1 m³ is 1,000 L, not 100 L. Length units scale by 100, area by 10,000 and volume by 1,000,000 between metres and centimetres.
