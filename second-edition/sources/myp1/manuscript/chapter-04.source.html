Chapter 4 · Mathematical development

4.1 Equivalent fractions and simplest form

Big idea

Multiply or divide numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number.

Fractions describe equal parts of one whole. 1/2, 2/4 and 4/8 name the same amount when the wholes are the same size. The number of pieces changes, but the covered amount does not.

To simplify, divide top and bottom by a common factor. A fraction is in simplest form when its numerator and denominator have HCF 1.

{{figure:fraction_bars-4|Three equal wholes. The same amount is shaded in each bar.}}
Worked example · Simplify 36/48

Find a common factor, or divide by the HCF.

  1. HCF(36,48) = 12.
  2. 36÷12 = 3 and 48÷12 = 4.
3/4.

4.2 Comparing and ordering fractions

Big idea

Use a common whole, a common denominator, a benchmark or a common numerator.

With equal denominators, more parts means a larger fraction. With equal numerators, more parts means each part is smaller. For 3/7 and 3/5, fifths are larger than sevenths.

For unlike parts, use a common denominator: 3/4 = 9/12 and 5/6 = 10/12, so 5/6 is larger. A benchmark such as 1/2 can make comparison faster.

Worked example · Order 2/3, 3/5 and 7/10

Use denominator 30.

  1. 2/3 = 20/30; 3/5 = 18/30; 7/10 = 21/30.
3/5 < 2/3 < 7/10.

4.3 Adding and subtracting fractions

Big idea

Make the parts the same size before combining or comparing amounts.

For like denominators, add or subtract numerators and keep the denominator. For unlike denominators, first write equivalent fractions with a common denominator.

Mixed numbers can be handled as whole and fractional parts or as improper fractions. Borrowing one whole means borrowing denominator/denominator, not 1/10.

Worked example · Add 3/4 and 5/6

Use denominator 12.

  1. 3/4 = 9/12 and 5/6 = 10/12.
  2. 9/12 + 10/12 = 19/12.
19/12 = 1 7/12.

4.4 Multiplying and dividing fractions

Big idea

Use “a fraction of” for multiplication and “how many groups?” for division.

To multiply fractions, multiply numerators and denominators: a/b × c/d = ac/bd. An area model shows why: a rectangle split into b by d equal parts has ac shaded parts.

Dividing by a nonzero fraction asks how many of those fractional groups fit. Dividing by c/d is multiplying by d/c. For example, 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 6.

{{figure:fraction_area-4|The overlap is 6 of 15 cells: 2/3 × 3/5 = 2/5.}}
Worked example · Find 2/3 of 3/5

Use a rectangle split into thirds one way and fifths the other.

  1. The whole has 15 equal cells.
  2. The overlap has 2 × 3 = 6 cells.
  3. 6/15 simplifies to 2/5.
2/3 × 3/5 = 2/5.

4.5 Decimal place value, ordering and rounding

Big idea

Read place value, compare from left to right and round only at the requested stage.

Digits right of the decimal point represent tenths, hundredths, thousandths and so on. Trailing zeros can record precision: 0.40 and 0.4 have equal value but different stated precision.

Compare equal place values from left to right. To round, inspect the next digit: 0–4 rounds down, 5–9 rounds up. Keep trailing zeros when they show the requested decimal places.

Worked example · Round 0.995 to two decimal places

The third decimal digit is 5.

  1. The hundredths digit 9 rounds up and carries through the tenths digit.
  2. Write two digits after the point.
1.00.

4.6 Fractions, decimals and measurement

Big idea

A fraction bar means division; choose the representation that makes the task clearer.

To convert a fraction to a decimal, divide numerator by denominator, or rewrite with denominator 10, 100 or 1,000 when possible. 3/8 = 0.375.

A reduced fraction terminates exactly when its denominator has no prime factors except 2 and 5. Otherwise its decimal repeats: 1/3 = 0.333…. Use an exact fraction when a rounded decimal would hide the value.

{{figure:number_line-4|A common scale makes 3/8 = 0.375 and 0.4 directly comparable.}}
Worked example · Compare 3/8 inch and 0.4 inch

Convert to the same representation.

  1. 3/8 = 0.375.
  2. 0.4 = 0.400.
0.4 inch is larger by 0.025 inch.
