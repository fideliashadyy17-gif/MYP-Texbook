# Exercise references: analysis and adaptation

The nine two-page papers below were reviewed as exercise references, not as the curriculum or style sheet. I inspected the rendered pages as well as the extractable questions. Figures are part of the papers themselves; no separate first-edition diagram asset set or editable first-edition authoring files were found. The old second-edition files were not used as teaching content, an exercise bank, or a rendering source. There is **no MYP1 assessment PDF** in the repository.

## What transferred, and what did not

- **Structure:** A compact stem defines the data and units once; numbered multipart prompts move from reading or computing to explaining, verifying, generalising or recommending. Early-year pages usually pair two short tasks. Upper-year pages may use one deeper multistep problem, two complementary tasks or an investigation-style prompt with evidence and a conclusion. Tables and diagrams are supplied directly on the practice page; a student-produced graph is requested only when plotting is itself part of the skill.
- **Mathematics:** 2A/2B directly inform comparison, ratios, rules and chance in MYP2, and give a developmentally simpler approach for MYP1 (equal fractions, signed locations, survey overlap, elementary charts). 3A/3B directly inform MYP3 real numbers, indices, proof and measurement. 4A/4B/4C directly inform MYP4 Chapter 12 and support MYP2 and MYP5 data practice. 5A/5C directly inform MYP5 Chapter 4 and MYP4 Chapters 7–8. For concepts not in the matching paper, the first-edition section fixes scope; the mapped PDF contributes a task-design lens only. No exam question, exam logo, original data table, or figure was imported.
- **Visual grammar:** Copy the useful information hierarchy (small legend, clear axis unit, nearby statement and figure, distinguish adjacent and overlapping regions), not the compressed two-column test aesthetic. Render original vector marks at book trim; preserve the first-edition cover and page designs. Statistical tasks distinguish bars, density areas and accumulations; transformation grids use equal Cartesian scales; some abstract graphs intentionally use different x and y scales. Schematic figures give dimensions in the task or diagram and need not be measured by a ruler.
- **Criterion choices:** A is used for mathematical method and transfer; B for detecting or generalising a pattern and checking it; C where a comparison, proof, notation, representation or audit is central; D where a real constraint, prediction or decision drives the mathematics. A context or a single “explain” instruction by itself does not make every task a D or C assessment. The criterion labels are instructional emphases, not marks or formal IB rubrics.
- **Quality trade-off:** The papers’ short items are useful diagnostics, but their dense layouts and distractor/matching formats are less appropriate for open notebook practice. The added pages prioritise visible reasoning, written conclusions, separate worked answers and room to work. The papers are limited to selected term topics; they do not justify bringing later material into earlier first-edition sections.

The `source/myp*.txt` headers give the reference ID(s) for each added section. `coverage.csv` joins them to the original section, start page, insertion anchor and criteria.
